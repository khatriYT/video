# Research Report: What If Every Vulture Disappeared?

**Compiled:** 2026-11-26.

Source IDs (S01–S16) refer to `source_library.md`. Claim-by-claim status is in `claim_checklist.csv`.

## 1. Why this topic works

Vultures are the classic "disgusting, useless" animal. They are also the rare case where the hypothetical **already happened at scale**: South Asia lost almost all of its *Gyps* vultures in about a decade (S02, S06). That lets the episode move from speculation to evidence:
- an unplanned natural experiment;
- a mystery: what killed them?
- a cascade: carcasses, flies, dogs, rabies;
- a measurable human cost (S01);
- a second crisis now unfolding in Africa (S11).

## 2. What vultures do

- **Carcass removal.** Specialist scavengers find and consume large carcasses fast. Excluding vultures slows decomposition:
  - about 2× slower in Costa Rica (S09);
  - nearly 3× slower in Kenya (S10).
- **Disease firebreak.**
  - Vultures' gut chemistry and microbiome let them process meat carrying pathogens (S13).
  - When vultures are absent, flies nearly double (S09) and mammal scavengers at carcasses roughly triple (S10). Mammals like dogs and jackals can carry and spread disease, rabies included.
- **Free waste management.**
  - Where vultures are missing or carcass disposal is forced elsewhere, humans pay in trucks, fuel and emissions. Spain: about 77,000 t CO2-eq a year (S12).
- **Cultural service.**
  - Parsi sky burial at the Towers of Silence depended on vultures. Communities have turned to solar concentrators, which work poorly in the monsoon (S15).

## 3. The South Asian collapse (the natural experiment)

| When | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1990s | *Gyps* vultures begin crashing across India, Pakistan and Nepal | S02 |
| By early 2000s | Declines of more than 95% in about 10 years | S02 |
| 2004 | Oaks et al. identify diclofenac, a cheap veterinary painkiller, as the cause. Vultures eating treated carcasses die of kidney failure. The finding is confirmed across the subcontinent | S03, S04 |
| 2006 | India bans veterinary diclofenac. Enforcement is patchy; human-use vials are diverted | S05 |
| Up to 99.9% | Estimated decline of the white-rumped vulture over two decades (BNHS) | S06 |
| 2023 | India bans ketoprofen and aceclofenac for animals (aceclofenac turns into diclofenac inside cattle) | S05 |
| 2025 | Conservationists say populations are still not secure; drug bans alone are not enough | S06 |

## 4. Consequences: an evidence ladder

| Consequence | Evidence strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Carcasses persist longer | **STRONG** | Experiments S09, S10 |
| More flies at carcasses | **STRONG** (Costa Rica experiment) | S09 |
| More mammal scavengers (dogs, jackals) at carcasses | **STRONG** (Kenya experiment) | S10 |
| Feral dog numbers rose in India alongside the vulture decline | **MODERATE / DEBATED** | S07 (correlation), S01 ("suggestive"); causality debated |
| Rabies exposure rose (vaccine sales up) | **MODERATE** | S01 |
| About 500,000 extra human deaths (2000–2005) | **MODELLED ESTIMATE** | S01: a statistical comparison of districts, not a body count. Present it as "one study estimates" |
| Water contamination near carcass dumps | **MODERATE** | S01 |
| Higher emissions and cost of carcass disposal | **STRONG** (Spain) | S12 |
| Loss of the sky-burial tradition | **DOCUMENTED** | S15 |
| Other scavengers fully replace vultures | **NOT SUPPORTED** | Mammals and insects pick up part of the work, but slower and with more disease risk (S09, S10) |

## 5. Things the script must not say

- That vulture stomach acid is "pH 1". Measured values in studies of New World vultures were higher. Use "harsh stomach acid" only.
- That India had "tens of millions" or "40 million" vultures. The figure is not verified. Use "so common nobody bothered to count them".
- That the 500,000 deaths are certain. They are an **estimate**.
- That vultures alone cause rabies control. It is a chain with several links, some debated.
- Any exact India rabies death figure as fact. Estimates range from about 5,700 to about 20,000 a year. Use "around a third of the world's rabies deaths" (WHO about 35%).
- That Africa's vultures are "extinct". Several species are critically endangered and declining.

## 6. Recommended narrative

- **Observation plate:** one carcass site at the edge of an Indian village, shown at Hour 0 (vultures), then Day 1, Week 2, Year 1 and Year 10 without them.
- **Cutaways:**
  - the mystery and lab (diclofenac);
  - an economics map (the district comparison);
  - the Towers of Silence;
  - Spain's trucks;
  - Africa now.
- **Ending:** what is being done (drug bans, breeding centres), and a reframe: "the ugliest birds were holding a line we never saw."
