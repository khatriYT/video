# Scientific Research: What Would Happen If All Mosquitoes Disappeared?

Researched 2026-11-23. Most findings come from search-result summaries of the sources listed. Where only an abstract or secondary summary could be read, the finding is marked *(secondary)*.

**Tags:**
- **[FACT]** established scientific fact
- **[INTERP]** scientific interpretation
- **[HYPO]** hypothetical prediction
- **[UNCERTAIN]** open question or conflicting evidence

## 1. Basic biology
- **[FACT]** About 3,500 mosquito species have been described. *(Institut Pasteur)*
- **[UNCERTAIN]** Estimates of how many species bite humans vary widely, from about 100 to roughly 6–15% of species. These figures count "bites humans" differently. Script wording: "from around a hundred to a few hundred". *(Institut Pasteur; other secondary sources)*
- **[FACT]** Only females take blood meals, for the protein needed to produce eggs. Males (and females, for energy) feed on plant sugars such as nectar.
- **[FACT]** The main disease-vector genera are *Anopheles* (malaria), *Aedes* (dengue, Zika, yellow fever, chikungunya) and *Culex* (West Nile virus and others).
- **[FACT]** Larvae are aquatic. Most species filter-feed on detritus, microorganisms and organic particles. They develop in ponds, puddles, tree holes, containers and plant-held water (e.g. pitcher plants: *Wyeomyia smithii*).
- **[UNCERTAIN]** Total global mosquito numbers ("trillions") are a popular estimate, not a measured figure. The script says "trillions" without a precise number.

## 2. Human health burden
- **[FACT]** Malaria: an estimated **263 million cases and 597,000 deaths in 2023** (WHO World Malaria Report 2024). The 2025 report estimates 282 million cases and 610,000 deaths in 2024. The African Region carries about 95% of deaths, mostly children under five.
- **[FACT]** Dengue: 2024 set the global record, with **over 14 million reported cases** (WHO fact sheet: 14.6M cases and more than 12,000 deaths. A 2025 IJID analysis: 14.1M cases and 9,404 deaths).
- **[FACT]** Vector-borne diseases as a whole (not only mosquito-borne) make up more than 17% of infectious diseases and cause more than 700,000 deaths a year (WHO). **Don't attribute the whole 700k to mosquitoes.**
- **[INTERP]** Mosquitoes are widely described as the animal responsible for the most human deaths, because of the diseases they carry.

## 3. Ecological roles
### 3a. Food webs: water
- **[FACT]** Larvae are eaten by fish, dragonfly nymphs, aquatic beetles, other insect larvae and some tadpoles.
- **[INTERP/HYPO]** Most of these predators are generalists. Fang (2010) reports many scientists expect other organisms (e.g. midge larvae) to fill the niche quickly. *(secondary)*
- **[UNCERTAIN]** Small, specialised habitats (e.g. pitcher-plant pools) could be affected in ways that are harder to predict.

### 3b. Food webs: Arctic
- **[FACT]** Arctic mosquitoes emerge from snowmelt pools in dense seasonal swarms.
- **[UNCERTAIN]** Fang (2010) cites one estimate that migratory tundra-nesting bird numbers could drop by more than 50% without mosquitoes. Another researcher (Cathy Curby, in the same article) notes mosquitoes don't appear in bird stomach samples in high numbers, and that midges matter more. *(secondary)*

### 3c. Caribou
- **[FACT]** Insect harassment (mosquitoes and oestrid flies) affects caribou behaviour. Caribou move to higher elevations and snow patches with poorer forage, and rest and feed less. (USGS: "Mosquitos, forage and the future of Arctic caribou"; Univ. of Alaska Fairbanks; Culler et al., Dartmouth / Proc. R. Soc. B 2015, via National Geographic.)
- **[HYPO]** Without mosquitoes, caribou movement and feeding could change, with knock-on effects on predators and vegetation.

### 3d. Pollination
- **[FACT]** The blunt-leaf orchid *Platanthera obtusata* is pollinated mainly by *Aedes* mosquitoes. Lahondère et al., PNAS 2019/2020 (UW, Riffell lab): nonanal-rich scent attracts mosquitoes, and about 10% of feeding mosquitoes carried pollinia. A 1970s study in Wisconsin identified *Aedes communis* and geometrid moths as pollinators.
- **[INTERP]** Mosquitoes are not significant pollinators of food crops. Bees, flies, moths and others dominate.
- **Accuracy note:** cacao (chocolate) is pollinated by ceratopogonid **midges**, not mosquitoes. **Do not claim mosquitoes pollinate chocolate.**

## 4. Human and social consequences
- **[HYPO]** The end of malaria, dengue, Zika and similar transmission would save hundreds of thousands of lives a year and reduce the economic burden.
- **[INTERP/HYPO]** Some ecologists (quoted in Fang 2010) suggest mosquito-borne disease has slowed human settlement of some tropical regions. Removing it could increase development pressure. Framed in the script as "some ecologists have suggested". *(secondary)*

## 5. Could it actually happen?
- **[FACT]** A gene drive targeting the *doublesex* gene suppressed caged *Anopheles gambiae* populations:
  - small cages in 7–11 generations (Kyrou et al., Nature Biotechnology 2018)
  - large indoor cages, reaching full suppression 245–311 days after introduction (Hammond et al., Nature Communications 2021)
- **[FACT]** Neither has been released for population suppression in the wild as of the 2021 coverage reviewed. The current field status was not re-checked.
- **[INTERP]** The research consensus favours targeting a few vector species, not all mosquitoes.

## 6. Key uncertainties to keep honest in the script
1. How much birds, fish and bats actually depend on mosquitoes specifically, as opposed to insects in general.
2. How quickly other insects (midges, other flies) would fill the vacated niches.
3. Region-specific effects, e.g. the Arctic and isolated habitats.

# Addendum (v2, for the 12-minute cut)

## 7. How mosquitoes find people
- **[FACT]** Exhaled CO2 (about 4% of breath) is the long-range activator and attractant. Reported detection distances are about 10 m ("30+ feet"), from press coverage. The script says "several metres".
- **[FACT]** At closer range, skin and sweat odours (e.g. lactic acid) and body heat guide landing. A 2024 *Nature* study found infrared heat doubled host-seeking, detectable to about 2.5 ft (0.75 m). The script says "within the last few dozen centimetres... heat". CO2 detection gates the responses to heat and lactic acid (McMeniman et al., *Cell* 2014). [Rockefeller University](https://www.rockefeller.edu/news/7833-research-shows-combination-of-sensory-signals-draw-mosquitoes-in-for-a-bite/); [UW 2015](https://www.washington.edu/news/2015/07/16/uw-researchers-show-that-the-mosquito-smells-before-it-sees-a-bloody-feast)

## 8. Life cycle
- **[FACT]** The stages are egg, larva, pupa and adult. All immature stages are aquatic. Pupae don't feed and are mobile ("tumblers"). In warm conditions, development from egg to adult can take about 7–14 days (CDC general guidance).
- **[FACT]** *Aedes* eggs laid just above the waterline can survive drying out for months and hatch when flooded (CDC).

## 9. Predators of adult mosquitoes
- **[FACT]** Dragonflies, spiders, swallows, swifts and other aerial insectivores eat adult mosquitoes among other insects.
- **[FACT]** Bats: DNA studies of droppings generally find mosquitoes are a small share of wild bat diets. Moths and beetles are often more important. One Wisconsin study (Wray et al. 2018) found mosquito DNA in about 72% of little brown bat samples. That shows frequency, not volume. [PMC3795000](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3795000/); [UW–Madison](https://news.wisc.edu/study-bolsters-bats-reputation-as-mosquito-devourers/)
- **[UNCERTAIN]** The popular claim that a bat "eats 1,000 mosquitoes an hour" comes from lab feeding studies and is not representative of wild diets.

## 10. History
- **[FACT]** The French Panama Canal effort in the 1880s is commonly estimated to have cost about 22,000 lives, much of it to yellow fever and malaria. Under US control from 1904, William Gorgas's sanitation campaign (draining or covering standing water, screens, fumigation) eliminated yellow fever from the zone by about 1905–1906. [Linda Hall Library](https://www.lindahall.org/william-gorgas/); [Gavi](https://www.gavi.org/vaccineswork/yellow-fever-and-panama-canal)
- **[FACT]** WHO's Global Malaria Eradication Programme ran from 1955 to 1969. It relied heavily on DDT indoor residual spraying. It was abandoned in 1969 amid insecticide and drug resistance, funding gaps and other problems, and much of tropical Africa was never fully included. [PLoS Med 10.1371/journal.pmed.1000412](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000412); [CDC history](https://www.cdc.gov/malaria/history/index.html)

## 11. Modern control
- **[FACT]** *Wolbachia* (wMel) *Aedes aegypti* releases in Yogyakarta, Indonesia (AWED cluster-randomised trial, 2017–2020) gave a **77% reduction in dengue incidence** and an 86% reduction in hospitalisations. Utarini et al., *NEJM* 2021. [PMC8103655](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8103655)
- **[FACT]** Sterile insect technique and self-limiting genetically modified males are released so that matings produce no surviving offspring. Field pilots include Oxitec releases (e.g. Florida Keys, from 2021).
- **[INTERP]** Gene drives are designed to self-propagate across populations and national borders. Governance, reversibility and community consent are active debates.

## 12. Deep history
- **[FACT]** Mosquitoes are known from mid-Cretaceous Burmese amber, about 99–100 million years old (e.g. *Burmaculex antiquus*, Borkent & Grimaldi 2004).
- **[FACT]** A 46-million-year-old blood-engorged fossil mosquito from the Kishenehn Formation, Montana, preserved haem-derived porphyrins from blood (Greenwalt et al., *PNAS* 2013, doi:10.1073/pnas.1310885110).
- **[INTERP]** The mosquito lineage survived the end-Cretaceous extinction and the Pleistocene glaciations, given its Cretaceous fossil record and modern diversity.
