## 1. Vultures (recommended)

- **Working title:** *What If Every Vulture Disappeared? India Already Found Out*
  - Alternative: *The Bird Nobody Missed, Until 500,000 People Died* (only if the figure verifies; see risks).
- **Central question:** what happens when nature's clean-up crew vanishes?
- **Why viewers care:**
  - Vultures are seen as disgusting omens of death. The twist is that losing them was a public-health disaster.
  - It *already happened*. In the 1990s–2000s India's vulture populations collapsed, by more than 95% for some species *(to verify)*. The cause was diclofenac, a cattle painkiller.
- **Scientific angle:**
  - Carcasses pile up.
  - Feral dog numbers and rabies rise.
  - Rats and disease follow.
  - The Parsi "towers of silence" lose their function.
  - An economics study (Frank & Sudarshan, *American Economic Review*, 2024 *(to verify)*) estimated a large rise in human deaths in affected districts.
  - Africa's vultures are declining now, which makes the story timely.
- **Visual opportunities:**
  - One savanna or village-edge **observation plate** with a carcass (non-graphic, stylized) at Day 1 / Week 2 / Year 1 / Year 10.
  - Thermal-soaring sky shots.
  - A dog-population map animation.
  - A pharmacy shelf (diclofenac).
- **How it differs:**
  - The competitor has no species or ecology video at all.
  - Their "feral dog pack" beat is incidental; ours is a documented causal chain.
- **Risks and limits:**
  - Carcass imagery must stay stylized and non-gory for advertisers.
  - Mortality figures are **modelled estimates** and must be presented that way.
  - The cause chain (vultures → dogs → rabies) is well supported. Other links are less certain and must be tagged.

## 2. Bats

- **Working title:** *What If All Bats Disappeared? A Fungus Already Ran the Experiment*
- **Central question:** what would farms, forests and tequila lose without bats?
- **Why viewers care:**
  - Bats are feared (vampires, disease).
  - The twist: they are pest control worth billions, and losing them has measurable human costs.
- **Scientific angle:**
  - White-nose syndrome has killed millions of North American bats since 2006 *(to verify)*.
  - A 2024 study in *Science* (Frank) linked those die-offs to more insecticide use and higher infant mortality *(to verify)*.
  - Agave pollination and seed dispersal for tropical forest regrowth.
- **Visual opportunities:**
  - Cave emergence at dusk (great in 3D).
  - A cornfield observation plate at night: moths in, bats gone.
  - Echolocation visualisation.
- **How it differs:** a different time-scale structure from the competitor (nights instead of days). It is also a disease topic told without fear-mongering.
- **Risks and limits:**
  - COVID and bat-disease topics need careful, non-alarmist framing.
  - Economic valuations vary widely between studies.

## 3. Spiders

- **Working title:** *What If Every Spider on Earth Died Tonight?*
- **Central question:** who keeps the insects in check?
- **Why viewers care:**
  - Many people fear spiders. It is a huge search topic and an easy "fear to fascination" reframe.
- **Scientific angle:**
  - Spiders are estimated to eat 400–800 million tonnes of prey a year (Nyffeler & Birkhofer, 2017 *(to verify)*).
  - Agricultural pest control.
  - Spider silk science.
  - Food for birds.
- **Visual opportunities:**
  - Dew-covered webs at dawn (a strong 3D material showcase).
  - A garden observation plate filling with insects over weeks.
  - A macro "silk factory".
- **How it differs:** a pure ecology topic. It also has the most natural macro-3D showcase.
- **Risks and limits:**
  - "Insect plague / famine" consequences are speculative. There is **no real-world precedent** of a spider-free ecosystem, so the episode leans more on hypothesis.
  - Arachnophobic viewers may avoid the thumbnail.

## 4. Rats

- **Working title:** *What If Rats Went Extinct? Some Islands Already Know*
- **Central question:** would a rat-free world be better or worse?
- **Why viewers care:** a universally disliked animal, and a genuinely two-sided answer.
- **Scientific angle:**
  - Island rat eradications are real experiments: South Georgia was declared rat-free after a 2011–2018 campaign *(to verify)*, and seabirds rebounded.
  - Also: plague history, city food waste, and the role of lab rats.
- **Visual opportunities:** a city alley plate against an island seabird colony plate. Both evolve over time.
- **How it differs:** the only topic on the list where the answer is "partly better", which makes it a fresh twist.
- **Risks and limits:**
  - Must avoid implying extinction would be good everywhere: native rodents matter.
  - The competitor's plague-village title uses "It Wasn't the Rats.", so avoid that phrasing.

## 5. Ants

- **Working title:** *What If Every Ant Disappeared? (All 20 Quadrillion)*
- **Central question:** what do the most numerous insects actually hold together?
- **Scientific angle:**
  - Global ant estimate of about 20 quadrillion (Schultheiss et al., *PNAS*, 2022 *(to verify)*).
  - Seed dispersal (myrmecochory).
  - Soil turnover.
  - Predation.
- **Visuals:** an underground colony cross-section in 3D, and a forest-floor observation plate.
- **Risks:** few real removal precedents. Ecosystem-wide claims must be hedged.

## 6. Flies

- **Working title:** *What If Every Fly Died? Say Goodbye to Chocolate*
- **Scientific angle:**
  - Cacao flowers are pollinated mainly by tiny midges *(to verify)*.
  - Flies drive decomposition and are the second most important pollinator group.
- **Visuals:** a cacao farm plate, and a decomposition time-lapse kept stylized.
- **Risks:** the chocolate hook must not be overstated; other pollinators may partially compensate.

## 7. Earthworms

- **Working title:** *What If Earthworms Vanished? The Surprising Answer for North America*
- **Scientific angle:**
  - Soil structure and nutrient cycling.
  - The twist: many North American northern forests had no earthworms after the last ice age, and introduced European worms are harming them *(to verify)*.
- **Visuals:** a soil cross-section with a forest-floor plate above it.
- **Risks:** a lower-curiosity title; the hook depends on the twist.

## My recommendation

**Vultures.**
- It has the strongest documented cause-and-effect chain of any topic here.
- It is a real-world precedent with human stakes, and an "ugly animal turns out vital" reframe that fits the channel's core idea exactly.
- It has a natural fixed observation plate for the 3D time-lapse.

Bats is a close second and has a similar evidence base.
