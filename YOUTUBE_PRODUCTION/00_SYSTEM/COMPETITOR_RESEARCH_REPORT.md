**What sets it apart [ANALYSIS]**

1. **One mascot, many worlds.** The red-cap hero is instantly recognisable in every thumbnail, so the brand works even at thumbnail size.
2. **Serials plus standalones.** "The Supermarket" and "Lincoln High (School)" run as numbered parts that pull viewers from one video to the next. Standalone episodes test new settings.
3. **Constraint-based premises.** Each video has a confined place, a number and a rule, e.g. "30 days", "Room 214", "Flight 212", "Don't go outside".
4. **Very fast output with simple art.** Stick-figure characters keep each episode cheap to make, which is what allows a daily schedule.

### Performance relative to age [ANALYSIS]

- **The two series pilots carry the channel.** They have 4.6M and 2.6M views, which is more than all other videos combined. Pilot titles are self-contained and searchable ("Zombie Outbreak: 30 Days Locked Inside a Supermarket"). Later parts depend on viewers having seen earlier episodes.
- **Each serial part gets fewer views than the last.**
  - Supermarket: 4.6M → 702K → 328K → 208K → 164K → 51K.
  - School: 2.6M → 725K → 423K → 266K → 141K → 30K.
  - This is the normal shape of a serial. Each part feeds on the one before it.
- **Standalones** earn 86K–348K within 3–4 weeks. The best are short, cryptic, rule-based titles (#6, #10).
- **Long compilations** (30–47 min) do moderately well per upload. They are cheap to make from existing material.
- **The true-story episode** (#25, 30K in 2 weeks) is the closest thing on the channel to factual content. It is one data point, so no conclusion should be drawn from it.

## 3. Content strategy [ANALYSIS]

**Content pillars**

1. Zombie-outbreak survival in a familiar place (supermarket, school, hotel, train, plane).
2. Rule-horror or eerie mysteries ("Three blinks means goodnight", "Don't go outside").
3. Confinement or time challenges ("30 days", "100 days", "1 year").
4. Occasional true survival stories.

**How an ordinary premise becomes a video**

1. Start with a familiar place everyone knows.
2. Add a threat.
3. Add a constraint: a time, a number or a rule.
4. Show the hero adapting day by day.
5. End with a twist or cliffhanger that sets up the next part.

**What transfers to a science channel**

- A familiar starting point: everyone knows mosquitoes.
- A dramatic "switch flipped" premise: they all vanish tonight.
- A time-lapse structure: the morning after, one week, one year, ten years.
- One recurring narrator-character who explores each world.

## 4. Title strategy [ANALYSIS from 29 titles]

| Pattern | Examples | Share |
|---|---|---|
| First person ("I/My/Me") | "I Lived Inside a Tank for 100 Days" | ~45% |
| Specific numbers | 30 Days, Room 214, Flight 212, 438 Zombies, 98 Nights, 17-Year-Old | ~45% |
| Rules or commands | "Don't Go Outside", "Never Let the Tiger See Your Back", "Stay in Your Room" | ~20% |
| Two-sentence tension | "Every Passenger on Flight 212 Is Dead. My Seat Is Empty." | ~15% |
| Question, "Why", "How" | "Why Nobody Jumps Off This Ship", "How a 17-Year-Old Survived…" | ~10% |
| Serial tag | "(Supermarket, Part 4)" | ~40% |

- **Length:** usually 6–12 words, or about 45–75 characters.
- **Avoided:** clickbait capitals, emoji and exclamation marks.
- **Tone:** calm and ominous.

### Reusable title framework for our niche

- **Hypothetical plus timeframe:** "What If Every Mosquito Died Tonight?" or "The World 10 Years After the Last Mosquito"
- **Specific number plus stake:** "3,500 Species Gone Overnight: A World Without Mosquitoes"
- **Two-sentence contrast:** "Every Mosquito Is Gone. Something Else Is Starving."
- **Why plus counterintuitive claim:** "Why Killing Every Mosquito Might Not Be a Good Idea"
- **Searchable pilot form for the first video of any series:** "What Would Happen If All Mosquitoes Disappeared?"

## 5. Thumbnail analysis [VERIFIED observations from 29 thumbnails; principles are ANALYSIS]

- **Art:** the same 2D hand-drawn stick-figure style as the episodes. The hero's face is often a large close-up with an "o" mouth showing surprise.
- **Text:** 1–4 words in heavy rounded sans-serif capitals. Letters are white with a thick black outline, and one word is set in an accent colour: yellow, red, sky blue or pink.
- **Split panel:** "DAY 1 | DAY 30" before/after panels separated by a white diagonal bar. The second number is in red. This appears on about a third of the thumbnails.
- **Badges:** "PART 4" rubber-stamp labels, plus tilted "10K/50K SPECIAL" luggage tags.
- **Colour:** muted, desaturated backgrounds with one saturated accent, usually the hero's red cap.
- **Composition:** the hero sits at one side, about 30–45% of the frame. The threat or crowd fills the other side. Night scenes use dark navy with a single light source for contrast.
- **Emotion:** shock, unease and curiosity, never gore.

### Original thumbnail guidelines for our channel

1. Make our own mascot (see the style guide) the face of every thumbnail.
2. Use 2–4 words. Show the creature, or its absence, large.
3. Use our split panel, "WITH | WITHOUT" or "DAY 1 | YEAR 10", to show the ecological change. Pick a different divider than theirs, such as a torn-paper edge.
4. Our text treatment: cream (#FFF6E0) letters with a dark-brown outline, accent words in mustard (#F2B33D) or alarm red (#E0533A).
5. The background should be one readable environment: pond, tundra or bedroom.

## 6. Recommendations [ANALYSIS]

1. Launch with **searchable, self-contained "pilot" titles**. Pilots drastically outperform follow-ups on this channel.
2. Build a recurring **mascot-explorer** so the channel is recognisable at thumbnail size.
3. Run **soft serials**, e.g. "The Disappearance Files #1, #2…". Make each video stand alone; the competitor's numbered parts lose views fast.
4. Keep each video at **about 8–11 minutes**, matching the competitor's main episode length.
5. Use simple stick-figure-style art that can be produced at speed, but give it **our own identity** (see the style guide).
