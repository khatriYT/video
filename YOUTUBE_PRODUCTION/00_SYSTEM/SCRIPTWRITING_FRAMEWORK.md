## 1. What we take from the competitor [ANALYSIS]

| Competitor technique | Our version |
|---|---|
| Familiar place plus sudden threat ("30 days locked inside a supermarket") | Familiar creature plus sudden absence ("every mosquito dies at midnight") |

## 2. Target length
- Competitor main episodes run **8–13 minutes** [VERIFIED].
- **Our target is 7–10 minutes:** 1,100–1,500 words at about 150 wpm of narration. Never pad.

## 3. The 10-beat structure (adapt; don't apply mechanically)

| # | Beat | Time | Purpose | Tools |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The switch** | 0:00–0:20 | State the premise as an event that has already happened ("Tonight, at midnight…"). | Present tense, short sentences, a concrete scene |
| 2 | **The obvious reaction** | 0:20–0:35 | Voice what the viewer thinks ("Sounds perfect, right?") | Rhetorical question |
| 3 | **The open loop** | 0:35–0:50 | Scientists disagree. Promise to find out. | Tension between two experts |
| 4 | **Why this creature matters to us** | ~1 min | Stakes for humans, with numbers from a named source. | 2–3 statistics max, cited by organisation |
| 5 | **The surprise reframe** | ~45 s | A fact that flips the viewer's mental model ("most mosquitoes never bite you") | "But here's the twist…" |
| 6 | **Ecosystem tour, stop 1** | ~1 min | Lowest level of the food web (larvae, water) | Pip visits a place |
| 7 | **Ecosystem tour, stops 2–3** | ~2 min | Predators, migrations, plants | Evidence plus honest uncertainty ("nobody knows for sure") |
| 8 | **Effect on humans** | ~1 min | Benefits and unexpected downsides | A counterintuitive consequence |
| 9 | **Reality check** | ~45 s | Could we actually do it? Current science. | Separate the lab from the real world |
| 10 | **Resolution** | ~30 s | Answer the opening question in 3–4 vivid images, then reframe it into a bigger question. | Echo the opening line |

## 4. Style rules
- **Sentence length:** mostly 6–15 words, with an occasional 3-word punch. One idea per sentence.
- **Paragraphs:** 1–4 sentences, so each maps to roughly one storyboard shot.
- **Address the viewer** as "you" in the hook. Use "we" in the exploration.
- **Every 45–60 s,** include a micro-hook: "But that's not the strangest part." or "And this is where it gets complicated."
- **Concrete over abstract:** "a puddle inside an old tyre", not "aquatic microhabitats".
- **Honesty markers:** "one estimate suggests", "researchers aren't sure", "probably". Never present a prediction as fact.
- **Numbers:** round them and name the source in speech ("according to the World Health Organization").
- **Pacing:** spoken text stays below about 160 wpm. Leave a 0.3–0.6 s beat after a reveal.
- **No textbook voice:** no "In this video we will discuss…". The hook starts in the first second.

## 5. Script file conventions
- `SCRIPT.md` holds the full script with section headers, a [VISUAL] note per paragraph, and source notes.
- `SCRIPT_FINAL.txt` holds narration only, one paragraph per line, with no headers or production notes.

## 6. Pre-publish fact checklist
- [ ] Every number has a source in `TOPIC_SCIENTIFIC_RESEARCH.md`.
- [ ] Each claim is tagged in the research file as fact, interpretation, hypothesis or uncertain.
- [ ] No claim is stated more confidently than its evidence.
- [ ] The group is not treated as uniform (e.g. "mosquitoes" vs "disease-carrying species").
