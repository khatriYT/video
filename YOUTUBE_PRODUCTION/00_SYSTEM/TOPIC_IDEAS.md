# Original Topic Ideas (Phase 5)

None of these is guaranteed to perform. They are ranked by fit with what worked for the competitor: a familiar subject, a dramatic premise, strong visuals and a surprising reframe.

| No. | Topic | Main question | Why it is interesting |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mosquitoes | What would happen if every mosquito disappeared tonight? | Huge human stakes (malaria, dengue) plus a real scientific debate. Hidden roles: Arctic birds, an orchid pollinator. |
| 2 | Vultures | What happens when scavengers vanish? | A real case: India's vulture crash in the 1990s was followed by more feral dogs and rabies. A strong cause-and-effect story. |
| 3 | Earthworms | What if every earthworm vanished from the soil? | Invisible soil engineers. Also a twist: earthworms are *invasive* in some northern forests. |
| 4 | Bees | What if bees vanished, and is "we'd starve" true? | Myth-busting. Wind-pollinated staple crops vs. pollinator-dependent fruits and nuts. |
| 5 | Dung beetles | What happens if nobody cleans up the dung? | Australia's dung-beetle introduction story. Very visual. |
| 6 | Bats | What if bats disappeared overnight? | Pest control for farms; agave and tequila; seed dispersal. |
| 7 | Ants | What would happen if all ants disappeared? | Enormous total biomass; seed dispersal; soil. |
| 8 | Spiders | What if every spider on Earth died? | Insect population explosion; a fear-to-fascination reframe. |
| 9 | Rats | What if rats went extinct? | Rats in cities, labs and disease; ecological gap-filling. |
| 10 | Cockroaches | Why don't we just eliminate cockroaches? | Only a handful of the ~4,500 species are pests; forest decomposers. |
| 11 | Termites | What if termites disappeared? | Savanna engineers; mounds as oases. |
| 12 | Wasps | What if wasps vanished? | Natural pest control; fig wasps and figs. |
| 13 | Moths | What if every moth disappeared? | Night pollinators; caterpillars as bird food. |
| 14 | Flies | What if every fly on Earth died? | Pollination (including cacao by midges); decomposition. |
| 15 | Sharks | What happens to the ocean without sharks? | Predator-loss case studies. |
| 16 | Wolves | What did Yellowstone look like without wolves? | A famous (and debated) trophic-cascade story. |
| 17 | Fungi | What if fungi disappeared? | Decomposition stops; plant root partnerships. |
| 18 | Phytoplankton | What if plankton vanished? | Ocean food webs and oxygen production. |
| 19 | Snakes | What if every snake disappeared? | Rodent explosions and crop losses. |
| 20 | Krill | What if krill vanished? | Whales, penguins and the carbon pump. |

## Top 5 recommendations
1. **Mosquitoes** (chosen for the first production). It is the most-searched version of this question, has the highest human stakes, and comes with a ready-made twist.
2. **Vultures.** A real historical example lets us use "this already happened" storytelling instead of pure speculation.
3. **Bees.** Very high public interest, and the myth-busting angle sets it apart.
4. **Earthworms.** An underrated "useless-looking animal matters" story with a surprising twist.
5. **Bats.** Strong visuals (night, caves) and economic stakes (pest control).
